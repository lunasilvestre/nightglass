# INTSUM 2026/039 — Evasion Pattern Indicators

## 1. Scope and limits of this note

This note lists **patterns**, not parties. Nothing in it supports an assessment
about a specific vessel, owner or flag, and it must not be cited as if it did.
Each indicator below has lawful explanations, and several are entirely ordinary
commercial behaviour in isolation.

The value of the set is **conjunction**. One indicator is noise; several
co-occurring on one hull over one voyage is a lead worth referring onward.

## 2. Why AIS interruption is itself significant under EU measures

Two provisions frame this cell's interest and are worth citing directly rather
than paraphrasing from memory:

- **Directive 2002/59/EC, Article 6** requires ships calling at a Member State
  port and fitted with AIS to **maintain it in operation at all times**, except
  where international agreements or standards provide for the protection of
  navigational information.
- **Council Regulation (EU) 2023/1214**, amending Regulation 833/2014,
  introduced measures addressing vessels that **switch off or otherwise disable
  their shipborne automatic identification systems** in connection with certain
  cargoes, alongside a requirement to give the competent authority advance
  notice of **ship-to-ship transfers** occurring within a Member State's
  exclusive economic zone.

The consequence for drafting is precise: in EU waters, an unexplained AIS
interruption is not merely a data quality problem. It is potentially conduct with
a regulatory character of its own, which is why it is worth reporting even when
nothing else about the voyage is known.

## 3. Indicator set

**Transmission behaviour**
- Repeated AIS interruptions on the same leg of the same route.
- Interruption beginning shortly before, and ending shortly after, a period
  spent within another state's exclusive economic zone.
- Presence established by an independent sensor during an interruption.

**Movement**
- Ship-to-ship transfer at sea where a port transfer was available and cheaper.
- Transfers conducted outside designated areas, or at night, or both.
- Loitering at sea with no subsequent port call.
- Deviation from the great-circle or customary route without weather or traffic
  explanation.

**Declared data**
- Declared draught inconsistent with declared cargo state.
- Draught changes across a period with no port call to explain them.
- Static fields inconsistent with measured hull dimensions.

**Documentary**
- Frequent changes of name, flag or registered owner over a short period.
- Insurance or class cover that lapses or changes at the same time as a route
  change.

This cell holds no documentary data. The last group is listed so that referrals
can request it, not so that it can be assessed here.

## 4. Handling

An assessment built on this set states which indicators were observed, which
were checked and absent, and which could not be checked at all. The third
category is usually the largest and omitting it inflates the finding.

Source reliability **B**, information credibility **2**. The regulatory
provisions cited are matters of published law; the pattern set is doctrine.
